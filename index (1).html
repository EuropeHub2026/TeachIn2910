The rules

How 53.7% of the vote became 56% of the seats

  • 250 seats, one nationwide constituency, closed party lists
  • d'Hondt method: each list's votes are divided by 1, 2, 3 … and the 250 largest results win seats
  • 3% threshold of votes cast; lists below it win nothing
  • National minority lists are exempt, and their quotients are raised by 35%
  • 126 seats for a majority

Was it fair?

A real choice, on an uneven playing field

Choice

Voters had a genuine choice

High turnout and strong competition between two main contenders. Preparations were efficient and voting was assessed positively at most stations.

1,485
polling stations observed
Media

Coverage tilted to the ruling side

Public and private broadcasters favoured United Serbia and mostly portrayed the Student List negatively. Threats to journalists fed self-censorship.

Resources

Incumbency advantages

Observers cited misuse of public resources, pressure on voters and disrupted campaign events. Online campaigning was largely unregulated.

Safety & access

Violence and barriers on the day

Physical attacks on polling staff, observers and journalists raised serious concerns.

What happens next

Four steps to a new government

1 · Sunday 1 November

Re-run at 53 polling stations

RIK repeats the vote where irregularities were found; final results follow.

2 · Within 30 days

New Assembly meets

Final results start a 30-day clock for the constitutive session.

3 · Then

Prime minister and cabinet

The Student List can govern alone. No prime minister has been named; Srdanović topped the ballot but resists being cast as leader.

4 · Timing to confirm

Presidential election

Vučić resigned the presidency in September; Speaker Ana Brnabić is acting president.
